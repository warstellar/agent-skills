# Brand naming to handoff

Develop a distinctive brand that works on the product's actual surfaces. Keep these instructions, references, and skill metadata in English. Discuss the work with this user in Russian; write customer-facing copy, mockup text, and external handoff documents in English, using sentence case. Preserve proper names and acronyms.

## Establish the starting point

Recover the product's job, audience, primary use case, business context, public positioning constraints, chosen names, rejected approaches, required surfaces, and delivery format from the conversation and available files. Distinguish user decisions, assistant proposals, and unknowns. Read referenced conversations with an available tool; disclose inaccessible messages and images rather than reconstructing them from imagination.

Do not repeat questions already answered by the sources. Proceed when the inputs are sufficient, labeling assumptions about optional gaps. If the name is chosen, resume at visual exploration or handoff. Reopen naming only when requested or when a material new constraint warrants it.

For Daymoon / the AI-readiness checker, read [references/daymoon.md](references/daymoon.md). Skip that reference for other products: its palette, metaphors, and business constraints are project-specific.

## 1. Naming to shortlist

Write a short brief: who the product serves, the concrete job it performs, and the impression it should leave. Explore several semantic territories instead of producing endless synonyms for one metaphor. An independent brand name can be paired with a descriptor that explains the category.

Reduce the options to a manageable shortlist, usually 3–5 unless prior decisions call for fewer. For each name, explain pronunciation or likely reading, associations, product relevance, visual potential, and the main risk. Compare memorability, distinctiveness, clarity in context, credibility, and suitability for the required surfaces. Avoid arbitrary numerical scores.

Use current search when checking name collisions, domains, or accounts; record links and the date. Distinguish a search observation, technical domain availability, and legal clearance. If unchecked, say so instead of claiming a name is available or ownable. Research does not authorize purchases or registration.

## 2. Independently discover visual directions

Use a separate subagent for each variant when delegation is supported. Each author independently discovers an art direction, studies references, and writes their own prompt. Do not assign palettes, compositions, or metaphors in advance to manufacture diversity.

Give each author a fresh, minimal context: the same product brief, fixed name or agreed candidate, actual constraints, common surfaces, customer-facing copy, and return format. Do not share the coordinator's preferred visual solution, sibling concepts, prompts, or images. Use a fresh/no-history mode where available, and do not claim isolation the tool cannot provide. Schedule work in parallel or sequentially according to tool limits.

Before delegating, read [references/independent-directions.md](references/independent-directions.md) for the assignment contract, return format, similarity check, and fallback. Hold the name constant when comparing art directions for one name. When comparing different names and visual systems together, disclose that both variables changed; do not attribute the preference to one variable without further evidence.

Collect independent concept cards before expensive rendering. Compare the central image or metaphor, composition, typography, materials or imagery, and relationship to the product UI. Swapping the name, color, and font on the same SaaS template does not create distinct brands. If concepts converge, return one for independent exploration with feedback about insufficient differentiation, without prescribing a solution or revealing the sibling concept. Then let each author realize their accepted concept.

## 3. Realize and compare

Use comparable conditions: the same product, copy, scenario, dimensions, and level of finish. A hero, a key product surface, and a small icon are often useful; select only surfaces relevant to this assignment. A shared task does not require a shared layout.

Use available image generation for visual concepts. For precise text or UI, a local HTML mockup or another appropriate format can complement the images. Do not turn brand exploration into production implementation. Observe tool contracts and concurrency limits. Without generation tools, provide concept cards and exact prompts, explicitly marking visual comparison as pending.

Save each final prompt, references, tool, exposed model information, dimensions, and result identifier alongside the variant. Do not invent a hidden model name. Open the resulting images or renders, inspect icons at their intended small size, and check text at the intended scale. Assess the visible result, not merely the prompt.

Show variants together with neutral labels. Compare distinctiveness, product fit, trust in the information shown, readability, small-size performance, and applicability to required surfaces. State each variant's strength, tradeoff, and unresolved risk. Make a recommendation grounded in visible evidence, keeping it separate from a confirmed user choice. Stop unnecessary iteration after a decision is made.

## 4. Handoff

Make the chosen direction usable by someone who has not read the chat. Adapt [references/handoff.md](references/handoff.md) to the assignment rather than producing a full brand book by default.

Deliver the brief and shortlist if those stages were needed; independent concept cards with references and prompts; available visual artifacts; a comparison and recommendation; the selected direction's handoff; and open decisions. Reuse existing work. Verify file links. Distinguish a concept image from a production logo, finalized brand tokens, or working UI.

## Anti-patterns and completion checks

- **The brands look the same.** Check visual grammar before rendering and actual artifacts afterward. More images using the same structure do not fix the problem.
- **The coordinator invents every author's direction.** Delegate the common brief and constraints; independent discovery and prompt authorship belong to each subagent.
- **A prompt is presented as a result.** Show artifacts and disclose failed details. An inaccessible render has not been inspected.
- **The image overshadows the product.** Inspect the key functional surface and trust in its information, alongside the hero. For an analytical checker this may be a report; do not add reports to products that do not need them.
- **A draft becomes an approved brand book.** Separate proposed fonts, colors, copy, and unchecked claims from decisions.
- **Daymoon becomes the universal style.** Apply its constraints only to that project.

The first two patterns come from explicit user feedback in the source and follow-up discussion. The others are workflow safeguards derived from the task, not quotations attributed to missing history. Before finishing, confirm distinct directions, English customer-facing copy in sentence case, preservation of the chosen name, clear fact/assumption boundaries, a usable handoff, and disclosed source and artifact limitations.
